2. Trend 1: Integration of Multi-Objective Optimization with Bilevel Reward Composition
2.1 Rationale and Current Gap
Humanoid robot control inherently involves multiple, often conflicting objectives—forward velocity, energy efficiency, balance, motion naturalness, safety, and joint limit avoidance—that must be simultaneously optimized [5], [6], [7]. Current bilevel optimization approaches for reward discovery, such as the regret minimization framework proposed by Lu [35], treat the reward function as a single scalar signal, collapsing the multi-objective nature of the problem into a weighted sum before optimization. This scalarization is known to be limiting: Jackson and Channon [5] demonstrated that the relative weighting and temporal structure of reward components dramatically affect emergent behavior, with combined enhancements producing gaits that are "much more robust to noise in their actions, with median speed, distance and time two to five times those of the default."

Berducci et al. [25] introduced Hierarchical Potential-Based Reward Shaping (HPRS), which enforces a natural order among safety, target, and comfort requirements, demonstrating that "by automatically balancing competing requirements, HPRS produces task-satisfying policies with improved comfort and without manual parameter tuning." However, HPRS does not employ bilevel optimization and does not explicitly address the multi-objective Pareto frontier. Multi-objective RL (MORL) has been studied independently [25], but its integration with bilevel reward optimization remains unexplored.
